# Add Director-Style Cinematic Lighting with Relight

![](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/s479xw3z/production/afcb13e49d9a8ae790535adec1a09b4ab6d36503-1696x960.png)Mariam BarovaJan 6, 20268 minutes

![Relight](https://higgsfield.ai/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=webp,onerror=redirect,width=1920,quality=85/https://cdn.sanity.io/images/s479xw3z/production/65ddd6ff5843fb6790edfba30541a159e219a69c-7200x4074.webp)

In digital photography, lighting is one of the most defining factors of image quality. Historically, correcting poor lighting required complex studio setups or hours of skilled post-production work. Today, Higgsfield is changing that paradigm.

Introducing **Relight**, a sophisticated AI-driven tool now available on the Higgsfield platform. Designed to give you complete control over image illumination, **Relight** leverages advanced generative AI and depth-mapping technology to manipulate light sources in a 3D space. It allows you to adjust angle, intensity, and temperature with precision essentially reconstructing how light interacts with your subject.

## **Created for the Wide Range of Practical Applications**

Relight isn't just a standard "add brightness" filter, it _understands_ the 3D geometry of your image and perfectly adjusts the lightning angle.

## **What is it best for?**

**1\. Save "Ruined" Portraits**

Did you take a selfie with the sun behind you? Use Relight to cast a **Front** light on your face. The AI smartly balances the exposure on the subject without washing out the background.

**2\. Professional Product Photography**

Flat lighting makes products look cheap. Add a **Top** or **Side** light to create depth and texture, making your product pop off the screen.

**3\. Creative & Artistic Effects**

Want a cyberpunk look? Change the **Light Color** to neon pink or blue. Want a moody noir vibe? Switch the light setting to **Hard** and cast deep shadows from the side.

## **Focus on Precision & Workflow**

Relight was invented to control the panel - to eliminate the guesswork typically associated with post-production lighting. Every element, from the granular brightness slider to the specific hex-code color input, is built for precision. Whether you are making subtle adjustments to color temperature or completely redefining the light source with the 3D directional pad, the interface provides a streamlined workflow that places professional-grade studio controls directly at your fingertips.

## **Key features:**

## **1\. The Quick Select Grid**

At the very top, you’ll find six rapid-fire presets: **Top, Front, Right, Left, Back,** and **Bottom**.

- **What it does:** Instantly snaps the light source to a standard studio position.
- **Pro Tip:** Use **"Front"** for standard beauty lighting or **"Back"** to create a dramatic "rim light" or halo effect around your subject’s silhouette.

## **2\. The Interactive Light Direction Pad**

This is the heart of the Relight feature. In the center of the panel is a dark control pad featuring a 3D light cone icon.

- **How to use it:** simply **click, hold, and drag** anywhere in this box.
- **Visual Feedback:** As you move your mouse, the light cone rotates in real-time, giving you immediate visual feedback on the angle and direction of your new light source. This allows for precise "off-axis" lighting that presets can't capture - like placing a light slightly above and to the left for a classic "Rembrandt" look.

## **3\. Light Quality: Soft vs. Hard**

Directly below the pad, a toggle switch lets you define the texture of the light.

- **Soft Mode:** Simulates a large light source (like a softbox or window light). It wraps around the subject gently, smoothing out skin textures and creating gradual shadow transitions.
- **Hard Mode:** Simulates a small, intense light source (like the sun or a flash). It creates crisp, defined shadows and high contrast, perfect for edgy fashion shots or dramatic storytelling.

## **4\. Fine-Tuning: Brightness & Color**

Once the position is set, you can refine the atmosphere with two precision controls:

- **Brightness Slider:** A granular slider (0–100%) that controls light intensity. It defaults to 50%, giving you room to either dim the light for a subtle effect or boost it to completely illuminate a dark scene.
- **Color Input:** By default, the light is neutral white (#FFFFFF). However, clicking the color swatch allows you to introduce creative lighting. You can manually enter a Hex code or pick a color to simulate environmental lights—like the orange glow of a streetlamp or the cool blue of a TV screen.

## **How to Use Relight: A Step-by-Step Guide**

Ready to try it out? Follow these simple steps inside **Higgsfield**:

1. **Upload your image:** Open **Relight** and load the photo you want to enhance.
2. **Navigate through the Relight tab:** Notice the control panel on the sidebar.
3. **Set your direction:** Start by clicking **Front** to illuminate the subject, or drag the manually control the pad to find a unique angle.
4. **Refine the mood:** Toggle between **Soft** and **Hard** light depending on the vibe you want.
5. **Generate:** Once you are happy with the settings, click the yellow **Generate** button. In just a few seconds, Higgsfield’s AI will re-render your image with your new lighting setup.

* * *

## **Conclusion: Professional Director Tools are Now Available to Everyone**

Relight turns lighting from a limitation into a creative control, because instead of settling for whatever illumination you captured in the moment, you can rebuild the light as if you were working in a studio and make intentional choices about direction, softness, intensity, and color.

Whether you are rescuing a backlit portrait, giving a product image the depth it needs to feel premium, or pushing an artistic look with bold colored light, the workflow stays fast and precise, since you can start with a preset for speed and then fine-tune the result with the directional pad and detailed controls. The end result is a cleaner, more professional image that matches the mood you actually want to deliver, without the time cost of complex retouching or the constraints of a physical lighting setup.
