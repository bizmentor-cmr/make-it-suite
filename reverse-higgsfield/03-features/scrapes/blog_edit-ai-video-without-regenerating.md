# How to Change One Element in a Finished AI Video Without Regenerating It

HiggsfieldSep 16, 202610 minLast updated: 1w ago

![Element Change ](https://higgsfield.ai/cdn-cgi/image/fit=scale-down,format=webp,onerror=redirect,width=1920,quality=85/https://cdn.sanity.io/images/s479xw3z/production/bbc95295540675f60f66bf617f1340becb121853-2720x1536.png)

To change one element in a finished AI video without losing everything else, point directly at what needs to change. That could be an object, a region, or a movement or performance. Describe the replacement, and the rest of the frame stays exactly as rendered, camera, lighting, timing. That's the difference between editing a clip and regenerating it from scratch.

## Why Full Regeneration Can Create New Problems

Run the same model with the same prompt twice, and you rarely get the same clip back. The character might look slightly different, the camera might frame the shot from another angle entirely. Nothing about the prompt locks in an exact repeat. That's fine when you're still exploring ideas. It's a different story once a clip is finished and approved, and only one small thing needs fixing. At that point, regeneration is the only option, and it can come back with a different camera drift, an expression that's off, or the opening moment changes.

| What Gets Regenerated | What's at Risk |
| --- | --- |
| Camera movement | A different drift, angle, or push-in than the approved take |
| Pacing and tempo | Timing shifts that throw off the edit around it |
| The actor's expression | A smile, a glance, a reaction that doesn't match the original |
| The hook | The exact opening moment that made the ad work in the first place |

Why Full Regeneration Can Create New Problems

Full regeneration can also cost more because every retry rebuilds the entire clip.

## How to Edit Elements in a Video on Higgsfield

**Higgsfield Genjutsu** covers this through two features:

- **Object Swap** replaces a specific object, a character, an outfit, a product, while everything else in the shot, the camera, the lighting, the motion, stays exactly as filmed.
- **Motion Transfer** takes the motion, camera work, and timing from an existing video and applies it to an entirely new scene built around a different subject or setting. Up to 30 reference images can guide either generation, alongside one source video up to 30 seconds. A prompt can be written directly to describe the change, or a preset can drive it without any text at all. Output goes up to 1080p.

**Seedance 2.5** Edit is better when you want to mark a region or describe a local change.

- The first is writing a prompt that describes the change directly, letting the model interpret where in the frame that change applies.
- The second is Draw to Edit, marking the exact region on the frame that needs to change first, then describing what happens inside that marked area, useful when the edit doesn't map cleanly to one named object.

Up to 50 references can guide a generation, alongside video up to 30 seconds. Output goes up to 1080p.

## Where You Still Regenerate

Local editing has limits. A few kinds of changes affect more than one local area at once, which means they usually need a new source performance or a full regeneration rather than a targeted edit:

- **Changing the character's underlying action or performance.** A different gesture, a different reaction, a different beat entirely, that's a change to the motion itself, not one region of the frame.
- **Changing the duration, pacing, or shot structure.** Extending a shot, cutting it short, or restructuring how it moves from one beat to the next touches the whole clip, not a single element inside it.
- **Replacing an object with something a very different size or shape.** The new object still has to move the way the original did, and a major mismatch in scale or shape breaks that fit.
- **Adding interactions that require new contact, movement, or occlusion.** A hand picking something up that wasn't there before, a character stepping behind an object, these need new physical interaction the source footage never had.
- **Changing several connected elements across the entire frame at once.** Once enough pieces are shifting together, camera, lighting, motion, and objects all at once, a full regeneration tends to hold together better than stacking several local edits on top of each other.

## Got any questions left?

Can I change one element in a finished AI video without regenerating it?

Yes. Object-level tools and region-marked local editing both change one part of a clip while leaving the rest untouched.

How do I swap a product in an existing AI video?

Use Genjutsu's Object Swap, or Seedance 2.5 Edit through a prompt or Draw to Edit, uploading the clip along with reference images of the new product. The camera, lighting, and motion from the original shot stay in place.

Can AI remove an object from a generated video?

Yes. Seedance 2.5 Edit's Smart Clean Up removes unwanted elements like dust, wires, or a specific object without manual tracking. Genjutsu's Object Swap can also describe removal in place of a replacement when the change fits that workflow.

Can I edit AI video inside After Effects?

Yes. Higgsfield's Adobe plugins include Draw to Edit for marking a specific area and Edit Video for prompt-based changes inside After Effects and Premiere Pro.

Can these tools edit real footage as well as AI-generated video?

Yes. Object Swap, Motion Transfer, Seedance 2.5 Edit, and Relight all work on real, filmed footage the same way they work on AI-generated clips, the source doesn't need to be AI-generated to begin with.

Why does the face change when I edit an AI video?

A full regeneration re-renders the entire frame, including the face, from scratch, so small variations creep in even when nothing about the face was meant to change. A targeted edit limits the requested change to another part of the frame, reducing the risk of changing the face.
